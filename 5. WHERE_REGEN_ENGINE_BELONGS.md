## Summary

Regen Engine is a private, experimental implementation of execution control
for declared constraints. Its evaluated **GS-LOCAL-0.1** path has been
exercised in local software tests and comparisons with a purpose-built
conventional baseline. The reported observations support specific claims
about the mechanisms under a trusted host. They do not establish protection
over an entire application.

The practical question is whether integrating Regen prevents relevant
failures, or reduces the work needed to prevent them, compared with competent
existing controls. **That benefit has not yet been established.** A useful
advantage could be additional protection, or comparable protection with less
integration, diagnosis or maintenance effort. Neither has been demonstrated
for a complete application in the record summarized here.

A post-hoc concurrency probe found a precaution already present in Regen
and absent from the first baseline. A small baseline repair removed that
observed difference. A separate request-binding comparison later reported
30/30 case executions passing on each variant. These are bounded observations,
not proof of general equivalence, superiority or lack of future utility.

## 1. The Problem Does Not Select the Implementation

An effect can occur before software discovers that it should not have been
permitted. Restoring a local snapshot may then leave the external consequence
untouched. Where the requirement is to prevent that consequence, recovery
after it occurs cannot, by itself, satisfy the requirement.

This motivates controls before the relevant effect. It does not establish
that a separate execution-governance kernel is necessary, that Regen is the
right implementation, or that every irreversible action requires additional
control. The unacceptable outcome and the required protection must first be
specified. Irreversibility alone is not a selection rule.

Existing controls may already suffice: resource-side authorization,
transactional operations, restricted capabilities, interlocks or approval
before commitment. Monitoring, recovery and retries can remain complementary
parts of the system. A comparison must include the controls the application
actually needs and can already use.

**Counter-example.** An assistant omits an attachment, and a follow-up message
fully corrects the omission. That failure gives no established reason to
integrate Regen. Likewise, even a costly failure does not justify another
kernel if an existing control already meets the requirement at lower cost.

Technical utility, novelty, adoption and commercial value are separate
questions. A familiar mechanism can be useful; a distinctive implementation
can still be unnecessary for a particular application. Neither test counts
nor architectural terminology establishes demand or a sale price.

## 2. The Mechanism and Its Actual Boundary

Here, **declared geometry** means the constraints represented to the execution
mechanism. It is a model of admissibility, not a measurement or certification
of the world. CP-Core data and declarations do not enforce execution merely
by existing in a container; an implementation and its integration must do so.
The broader ContinuumPort project, CP-Core and the evaluated GS path are not
interchangeable objects of evaluation.

The intended commit contract separates a proposal from permission to execute.
The local GS path checks the prepared request and relevant state, authority
and geometry at commitment. Advisory acceptance alone does not authorize an
effect. The recorded tests exercise selected parts of this contract; they do
not show that every relevant condition is checked on every execution path.

The evaluated setting trusts the host, implementation, adapter,
instrumentation, keys and authority store. The test actor uses the exposed
interfaces; it cannot modify those trusted components or run arbitrary code
outside the adapter's contract. The observed gate is therefore not a
demonstrated isolation boundary against arbitrary code in the host process.
Correct routing and restricted access to effect primitives are integration
preconditions, not conclusions supplied by the word "kernel."

Four limits remain explicit:

- **Path coverage.** Results for GS-LOCAL-0.1 do not automatically apply to
  older or unenrolled entry points. Retained diagnostics report different
  behavior on some historical paths.
- **Complete mediation.** No complete inventory and validation of every
  effect-capable path has been established. External I/O must be brought
  within the integration's enforcement boundary; this is not automatic.
- **State and external effects.** Restoring managed state does not undo a
  file write, network request or payment. Crash atomicity between authority
  changes and external effects has not been established.
- **Authority storage.** Complete rollback to an authentic older
  trusted-store snapshot remains accepted in the recorded diagnostic.
  Resistance to that rollback is not provided by the evaluated profile.

The linked
[execution-model limits](https://github.com/giorgioroth/ContinuumPort/blob/main/docs/EXECUTION_MODEL_LIMITS.md)
state assumptions including sequential deterministic execution and adapter
compliance. Empirical concurrency observations do not extend those proofs or
establish that the implementation refines every property of the model.

## 3. Existing Ideas and the Scope of the Contribution

Pre-execution enforcement has substantial prior art.

- **Complete mediation and least privilege.** Saltzer and Schroeder describe
  checking accesses for authority and limiting privilege. They also address
  cached authorization when authority changes. Applying that concern across
  a prepare/commit interval is an engineering obligation, not a new principle
  introduced here. [1]
- **Runtime enforcement.** Schneider characterizes a class of policies
  enforceable by mechanisms that monitor execution and stop prohibited steps.
  This literature supplies mechanisms and limits; it does not imply that any
  declared requirement can be enforced by a runtime check. [2]
- **Shielding.** Bloem and colleagues synthesize runtime shields that correct
  outputs to enforce specified properties of reactive systems. Regen is not
  claimed to implement their synthesis procedure. [3]

Complete mediation requires coverage of relevant accesses; it does not
require exactly one mediator. A single commit boundary is a Regen design
choice, not a theorem derived from these sources. A structural test counting
components can check that choice within its enumeration, but cannot alone
establish that every dynamic effect path is mediated.

The contribution presented for evaluation is a particular capsule/kernel
implementation, its declared contracts and the results of exercising it.
This document makes no novelty or priority claim for preventive control,
declared admissibility or an "irreversibility threshold." Failure to find an
identical formulation in a limited search cannot establish such a claim.

## 4. Reported Verification Record

The kernel is not included in the public ContinuumPort repository. Source
access, test execution and the associated archives have been handled
privately. This page summarizes maintainer-supplied records; it does not
publish a reproducible implementation release or independently authenticate
the underlying executions.

The supplied record describes a Windows run on **17 October 2026**, using
Python 3.14.4 and pytest 9.0.2:

| Evidence class | Reported result | What the result supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing pytest suite | 2,077 tests and 31 subtests passed | Those assertions passed in that recorded run |
| GS property suite | 20 tests passed | The selected GS cases passed |
| Injected-fault sensitivity controls | 15/15 faults caused assertion failures; zero test errors | Those tests detected those deliberately introduced faults |

These classes are not combined into a total number of guarantees. The
fifteen faults are matched to fifteen property tests; their detection is not
a measured detection rate for unknown defects. Five GS tests have no
corresponding injected fault in that set. Equivalent sensitivity controls
have not been demonstrated for all 2,077 existing tests.

The source-selection manifest and the reported absence of tracked source
changes help identify what the record concerns. They do not independently
authenticate execution or the clock. The Windows run was not reproduced for
this editorial update. It is separate from the comparisons and probe
calibrations discussed below.

Earlier test totals remain historical observations. They are not silently
converted into the current reported count or into evidence that protection
improved proportionally.

### Why repair history remains relevant

The author's earlier account describes a commit-boundary defect discovered
while the then-current suite remained green. Subsequent binding, ordering,
provenance and perimeter tests repaired gaps in that coverage.

The self-audit,
[*Evidence That Does Not Reach the Claim*](https://ssrn.com/abstract=7295418),
records that history. The repairs, later test count and self-audit do not
establish the earlier broad non-bypassability claim. A suite remaining green
through a known defect is a reason to evaluate the observer as well as the
implementation.

## 5. What the Comparisons Show

Development and review have used AI tools. Their code exposure and roles
differ across records; agreement among AI outputs is not independent
validation. In the comparisons below, Codex authored the conventional
baseline, the original cases and the baseline repair. Reviewers later
inspected code and proposed additional probes. Those probes are post-hoc and
non-blind. No independently authored baseline or held-out case set is part
of the evidence summarized here.

The baseline was purpose-built for the workflow. Treating it as a competent
conventional comparator is an evaluation assumption, not an independently
established fact about the best available alternative.

### 5.1 Synthetic managed transfers: one precaution, then a repair

The X01/X04 comparison used managed state with a budget and ledger. It
involved no bank, on-chain settlement or human payment mandate.

| Comparison | Reported observation | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ten sequential cases | Both variants satisfied the cases | No difference observed on this set; two cases use the same baseline write path |
| Post-hoc foreign-thread write during a paused commit | The original baseline admitted an unauthorized write; GS refused it | One concurrency failure under the probe's schedule |
| Same probe after baseline repair | Both variants satisfied the probe | A thread-owner check, four added lines and one removed line, removed the observed difference |
| Controlled concurrent commits of one receipt | Both variants satisfied the recorded repetitions | No general exactly-once, replay-resistance or distributed-correctness claim follows |

The unauthorized baseline write was observed before rollback restored the
final managed state. A final snapshot alone would have missed that event.
The comparison used an event oracle with trusted instrumentation; it did
not observe every effect primitive or isolate the subject from its host.

The narrow positive result is that Regen already contained a precaution the
first baseline omitted. The repair removed the observed difference. Its size
measures editing after discovery, not the cost of discovering the defect,
integrating either implementation or maintaining it. Those costs were not
measured.

### 5.2 Request binding: no difference on the declared cases

The maintainer's later comparison record reports a paired run on
**17 October 2026**, on Linux with Python 3.12.14 and `cryptography` 50.0.2:

| Recorded measure | Conventional baseline | GS-LOCAL-0.1 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Distinct cases | 20 | 20 |
| Case executions, including repetitions | 30/30 PASS | 30/30 PASS |
| Required legitimate-operation instances, including repetitions | 29 | 29 |
| Instances when each case is counted only on its first execution | 15 | 15 |
| Observed false refusals in this set | 0 | 0 |
| Injected-fault calibrations detected | 6/6 | 3/3 |

The reported GS interface smoke test preceded the scored cases. This paired
result was not rerun for the present revision. A separate earlier reviewed
package recorded a baseline-only run with GS blocked by missing source.
That package remains incomplete; the later paired result must not be
retroactively attributed to its different protocol.

Thirty executions are not thirty distinct cases. Repeating controlled
schedules is not exhaustive concurrency exploration. The operation counts
describe instances within this protocol, not independent kinds of operation.

The sensitivity evidence is asymmetric. The six baseline calibrations
included three native-baseline faults and three wrapper faults; GS received
the three wrapper faults. This comparison did not demonstrate sensitivity
to native GS faults in authentication, freshness or the thread gate. The
separate 15-fault suite above does not fill that gap in this comparison.

The additional `publication` event came from a shared wrapper. It was not
an independent observation of a variant-specific external publisher,
contract acceptance or blockchain transaction.

The baseline already passed the selected cases before the paired GS run.
This sets a ceiling for demonstrating better protection on that fixed
pass/fail score. Both passing supports **no difference observed on the
declared cases under the recorded conditions**. It does not establish
general equivalence or absence of practical benefit. Integration effort,
diagnosis, maintenance and performance advantages require separate evidence.

### 5.3 Local vault demonstration: attribution matters

A separate local demonstration concerned key rotation for a
contract-managed MultiversX vault, not replacement of a native account key.
The maintainer's record reports ten protocol-model scenarios, five detected
model faults, GS request-binding checks and ten contract scenarios executed
in the SDK's native Rust VM.

The contract applied its own revocation, epoch, nonce and signature rules.
The same contract scenarios also passed with identical vectors **without
GS**. The contract did not verify proof that a request had passed through GS.
The result therefore does not establish that adding Regen caused the
contract's protection or supplied an additional practical benefit.

The record does not include a built WASM artifact, Go VM execution or
transactions on a network. It does not demonstrate recovery after compromise
of the underlying signature scheme. These local results were not rerun for
this editorial update.

## 6. Open Findings Must Travel With the Results

### Probe calibration remains a separate problem

A later GS-only cross-thread probe has reported passing observations under
a single controlled schedule and pause point. Its revised observer checks
intermediate state rather than relying only on refusal exceptions and the
final restored snapshot.

Its calibration classifier nevertheless has a documented defect: it can
count an infrastructure failure as detection of an injected fault even when
the test never reaches fault installation. The preserved record contains a
reproduced counterexample; no validated repair is established in the records
used here. The calibration label is therefore insufficient on its own.

This is a defect of the probe, not a demonstrated GS defect. Specific
intermediate-state observations remain distinct from the faulty aggregate
label. They also do not establish coverage of all effect primitives or
interleavings; effects restored within one call or outside the observed
state can escape that observer.

### API robustness and contract-dependent results

Separate probes recorded undeclared API exceptions for malformed inputs in
both variants. No effects were observed in those cases. Under the later
explicit robustness criterion, those exceptions are not controlled refusals.
That later criterion does not retroactively change the original frozen set.
Likewise, zero observed false refusals in the request-binding set does not
establish support for arbitrary constraint expressions or inputs.

An append-target difference also remains contract-dependent: GS treats
`null` as an absent target in the evaluated path, while the baseline refuses
it under the evaluator's draft rule. The GS docstring describes a stricter
rule than its implementation. Consistency across code layers does not
establish intent.

The evaluator's **R-APPEND** rule is not automatically a normative integration
contract. If an integration adopts it, a violation matters even when another
permitted request can reach the same final state. The expected behavior must
be agreed before scoring this difference as an implementation defect.

## 7. External Reports Are Separate Evidence

The author's
[AP2 issue #309](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/issues/309)
records a source-level observation: the x402 PSP sample could skip mandate
verification when the agent-provider public key was unavailable, while other
verifying roles refused. The original report did not demonstrate an
unverified settlement or a successful unauthorized broadcast.

[PR #310](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/pull/310) reports a
direct reproduction reaching the binding step before the proposed fix and
an early missing-key refusal after it. The public GitHub record was checked
on **21 October 2026**: the PR remained open and unmerged. These records
support the described control-flow issue and proposed repair within their
stated limits.

Regen's kernel was not the enforcement mechanism in that AP2 flow. The
report is not a deployment test of Regen, an endorsement by AP2 maintainers
or evidence of commercial benefit from integrating it.

## 8. Where Further Evaluation Is Justified

A candidate setting is a bounded software workflow in which an integrator
can enumerate relevant effects, restrict access to their primitives, express
the required constraints and compare Regen with existing controls. Agentic
software is a convenient local test setting. That convenience does not
establish utility for a complete agent application.

The next useful evaluation needs one concrete application, a credible
alternative, an important benefit and a stopping rule:

1. **Fix the workflow and contract.** Define prohibited effects, legitimate
   work, authority changes, concurrency, external systems and acceptable
   failure behavior. Resolve contract-dependent expectations before scoring.
2. **Choose a realistic alternative.** Include the controls already available
   to the application. Declare authorship, implementation exposure,
   configuration work and repairs made after review.
3. **Observe the effects that matter.** Use observations that survive local
   state restoration. Calibrate with controls that actually install and
   exercise each intended fault. Separate harness errors from subject failures.
4. **Separate discovery from confirmation.** Freeze additional cases before
   exposure to results where feasible. Declare prior exposure and hints.
   Held-out cases improve independence of case selection; they do not create
   an independently authored baseline retrospectively.
5. **Measure benefit and cost.** Record unauthorized effects, incorrect
   refusals, recovery behavior, latency and integration and maintenance
   effort. Include discovery and diagnosis, not just final patch size.
6. **Choose the decision and stopping rules beforehand.** State the minimum
   useful benefit and the evaluation budget. If the evidence is inconclusive,
   identify what is missing rather than counting it as a win or a loss.

If existing controls meet the same requirement with less cost or complexity,
there is no demonstrated reason to add Regen for that workflow. If Regen
measurably improves protection or reduces the work needed to obtain it, that
supports a bounded utility claim for that integration. Neither outcome
settles every possible use or supplies a commercial valuation by itself.

## 9. Physical and Safety-Critical Domains Remain Hypotheses

Autonomous vehicles, launch and propulsion control, neural interfaces,
industrial robotics and medical systems can motivate questions about
preventive enforcement. No Regen hardware integration, domain-specific
validation, safety qualification or certification is established here.

A declared speed limit, stimulus range or pressure bound would not itself
establish physical safety. Sensor validity, timing, actuator behavior,
hardware faults and the appropriateness of the constraints require their
own evidence. This document supplies no deployment instructions or validated
numerical limits for those domains.

Validation and permission are separate. The published Beta license provides
for evaluation, research, non-commercial development and internal testing,
subject to its terms. Production use requires a separate commercial
agreement. Its Safety & Field-of-Use Notice requires **both** a separate
written agreement with the author **and** independent safety qualification
and regulatory conformity assessment by the integrator for the listed
high-risk uses. This page grants neither permission nor qualification;
the applicable license and governing separate agreement control. [4]

## 10. Present Position

Regen Engine currently belongs in bounded research and integration evaluation
for execution control, under explicit trust and effect-boundary assumptions.
The record includes local test results, a concrete precaution found in GS
before its addition to the baseline, later cases with no observed difference,
and unresolved implementation-contract and harness questions.

**Practical comparative advantage remains unconfirmed. Commercial value
remains undetermined.** The evidence establishes neither general conformance
of the implementation to its model nor a benefit for a complete application.
These limits do not establish that the project is useless; they identify
the question the next application-level comparison must answer.

An engineer must validate both the declared constraints and the integration
that applies them. An action allowed by a declaration can still be harmful
in the world.
